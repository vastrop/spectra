# A5V — A5 dwarf

## Class: A stars
A-type stars have surface temperature (Teff) ≈ 7500-10000 K and appear white to blue-white. Their optical spectra are dominated by the hydrogen Balmer series, which reaches its historical maximum strength at A0-A2 (the whole series from Hα 6563 Å through H9 3835 Å, plus the Balmer jump near 3646 Å continuum discontinuity). Ca II K (3933 Å) is weak at A0 but grows rapidly through the class, its strength relative to the neighboring Hε line (3970 Å) serving as the classic subclass clock from A0 to A9. He I lines, prominent in B stars, have disappeared by A0. The general metal-line spectrum (Fe I/II, Ti II, Cr II) is weak at A0 and strengthens steadily toward F, foreshadowing the metal-dominated G/K spectra. Mg II 4481 Å is the exception and should not be used as the example of an emerging metal line: it is already a working diagnostic in late B (paired with He I 4471) and changes comparatively little across the A class.

## This type: A5, luminosity class V (dwarf)
Mid-class: the Balmer lines remain strong and broad (dwarf gravity) but are visibly weaker than at A0-A2 as the temperature drops. Ca II K has grown to a moderate, clearly measurable strength relative to Hε — the K/Hε ratio here is a reliable subclass indicator. Mg II 4481 Å and Fe I metal lines are now consistently detectable, foreshadowing the F-star metal-line spectrum.
