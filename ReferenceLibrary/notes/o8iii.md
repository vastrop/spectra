# O8III — O8 giant

## Class: O stars
O stars are the hottest and most luminous of the classical spectral classes, with surface temperature (Teff) ≈ 30000-50000 K and a blue-white colour. The optical spectrum is defined by the He II/He I ionization balance: He II 4541 Å is the temperature clock: it weakens steadily from O5 toward O9 while He I lines (4471 Å, 4387 Å), weak at the earliest subtypes, strengthen toward the O/B boundary as the atmosphere cools. Equality of He I 4471 Å and He II 4541 Å defines roughly O7, and by O9 He I is clearly the stronger of the two. He II 4686 Å is present as well, but its depth is set by wind and luminosity as much as by temperature, so it is not a clean temperature indicator. Balmer lines (Hα, Hβ, Hγ) are present but comparatively shallow, diluted by helium lines and by strong stellar-wind effects at the earliest types. Some O stars show Of characteristics — N III 4634-4642 Å in emission, sometimes with He II 4686 Å partly filled by emission — signalling an extended, wind-fed atmosphere rather than a change in temperature. O stars are rare, short-lived, and born in young star-forming regions, though runaways can be found far from where they formed.

## This type: O8, luminosity class III (giant)
Mid-sequence subtype where the He I/He II balance has shifted noticeably from the earliest O types: He I 4471 Å is now stronger than He II 4541 Å — the two are equal near O7 — and the He II 4542/He I 4388 ratio has dropped below unity. He II 4686 Å is still in absorption, but its depth reflects wind and luminosity as much as temperature. The giant luminosity class gives intermediate line gravity relative to a dwarf or supergiant of the same subtype, and can show mild wind-related filling of the He II 4686 Å profile.
