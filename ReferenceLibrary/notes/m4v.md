# M4V — M4 dwarf

## Class: M stars
M stars are the coolest of the classical spectral classes reachable with amateur slitless spectroscopy, with surface temperature (Teff) ≈ 2400-3900 K and a deep orange-red colour. The class is defined by broad molecular absorption bands rather than atomic lines: overlapping TiO band systems chop the continuum into a saw-tooth pattern of shallow humps with steep blue-degraded edges, strengthening through most of the class as the atmosphere cools and more titanium oxide survives — though individual bands saturate or turn over late in the sequence, and their strength depends on luminosity and metallicity as well as temperature. In the coolest subtypes (roughly M6 and later) VO bands appear alongside TiO and add further structure. Atomic lines that dominate hotter stars survive only as islands between the molecular bands: Ca I 4226 Å, the Na D doublet near 5890/5896 Å, and the gravity-sensitive CaH (red) and CaOH (green) bands, which are strong in dwarfs and weak in giants. Hα near 6563 Å is normally a weak absorption line, or absent; it appears in emission in magnetically active dwarfs (flare stars, dMe), and phase-dependently in pulsating AGB giants, where shocks drive Balmer emission.

## This type: M4, luminosity class V (dwarf)
A cool dwarf, typically near or below the fully convective boundary (which is not a sharp subtype). TiO bands are deep and the continuum is strongly stepped between band heads; MgH and CaH remain strong gravity indicators, and this subtype is a common source of active dMe flare stars, so Hα emission (rather than absorption) is not unusual at this and later dwarf subtypes.
