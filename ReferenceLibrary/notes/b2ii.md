# B2II — B2 bright giant

## Class: B stars
B stars have surface temperature (Teff) ≈ 10000-30000 K and appear blue-white. He II lines are absent — this is what separates the B class from O — while He I lines reach their maximum strength around the mid-B subtypes (near B2) and fade steadily toward A as helium becomes harder to excite and hydrogen comes to dominate; the prime diagnostics are He I 4026 Å, 4471 Å, 4387 Å and 5876 Å, with the He I 4471/Mg II 4481 ratio taking over as the temperature clock toward late B. The Balmer series strengthens steadily through the B sequence toward its A-star maximum, with Hβ, Hγ, Hδ and Hα all visible and growing from B0 to B9. A sizeable minority of B stars are Be stars: rapid rotators with a gaseous circumstellar disc that produces Hα (and sometimes other Balmer lines) in emission rather than absorption, a phenomenon amateur slitless spectroscopy (e.g. Star Analyser 100) readily detects. Luminosity class in B (and A) stars is read largely from Balmer line width: broad wings mean high surface gravity (dwarf), narrow wings mean low surface gravity (supergiant). At B0-B2 hydrogen needs help — the O II blends near 4070, 4348 and 4416 Å and Si III 4553 Å carry much of the luminosity classification there — while from about B5 on the Balmer wings do the work at this resolution.

## This type: B2, luminosity class II (bright giant)
B2 sits near the He I line strength maximum for the whole B class (4026, 4471 Å at their most prominent). As a bright giant, surface gravity is intermediate: Balmer line wings are narrower than a dwarf but broader than a supergiant of the same subtype.
