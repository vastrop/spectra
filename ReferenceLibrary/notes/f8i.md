# F8I — F8 supergiant

## Class: F stars
F-type stars have surface temperatures (Teff) of roughly 5900-7500 K, bridging the hot white A stars and the cooler yellow G stars (like the Sun), and appear white to yellow-white. The Balmer lines (Hα, Hβ, Hγ, Hδ) are still fairly strong at F0 but weaken steadily through the class as the Ca II K & H lines (3933/3968 Å) grow rapidly, with the K line overtaking Hε (3970 Å) right around F0 and both H & K lines exceeding the Balmer lines in strength by mid-F. The CH G band (~4300 Å) appears around F2-F3 (depending on resolution) and strengthens toward the G class, while metal lines - the Fe I 4383 Å and Ca I 4226 Å blends - and the Mg b triplet (~5167-5184 Å) grow steadily, especially in the second half of the class. F-type supergiants occupy part of the classical Cepheid instability strip, giving the class an outsized role in distance-scale work.

## This type: F8, luminosity class I (supergiant)
Near the cool (red) edge of the class, F8 supergiants show a strong, well-developed G band and prominent metal lines, all narrow and sharp from very low surface gravity. Sr II 4077 Å is strongly enhanced, and this cool, luminous end of the F class overlaps the classical Cepheid instability strip alongside the earlier F5 supergiants.
