# K2V — K2 dwarf

## Class: K stars
K-type stars have surface temperatures (Teff) ≈ 3900-5300 K on the main-sequence scale (giants and supergiants of the same subtype run cooler), cooler than the Sun (G2V) and appearing orange. Ca II K & H (3933/3968 Å) are very strong, near their maximum along the spectral sequence in early K, though the line cores are also sensitive to chromospheric activity; Ca I 4226 Å is strong throughout. The CH G band (~4300 Å) keeps strengthening to a peak in early K — about K2 on the main sequence — and fades through the rest of the class as cooler, redder continua and increasing molecular/metal-line blanketing take over. The Mg b triplet (5167-5184 Å) and Na D doublet (5890/5896 Å) are strong. CN bands (4150-4215 Å) are strong in giants and weak — but not absent — in dwarfs. Balmer lines are weak, with Hα the only line still readily visible; in G8-K5 supergiants they are nonetheless stronger than in a giant or dwarf of the same subclass, which is itself a luminosity clue. TiO onset depends on luminosity: around K5 in giants, but not until very late K/M0 in dwarfs, foreshadowing the M class that follows.

## This type: K2V
K2, luminosity class V (dwarf), Teff ≈ 4850-5000 K. High surface gravity keeps the Mg b triplet, Na D, and the MgH band near 4780 Å strong and broad; CN (4150-4215 Å) is weak — much weaker than in a giant of the same subclass, but not truly absent — and the Ca II infrared triplet (8498-8662 Å) is comparatively weak and broad next to a giant of the same subclass.
